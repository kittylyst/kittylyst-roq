One of my first disgruntlements with the English language was our unnecessary overloading of the concepts of "gratis" and "libre" with the single English word "free".

It is true that "libre" can imply "gratis" - for example, if you are unfortunate enough to live in a capitalist society, then a public-spirited person can always supply what is "libre" at zero cost, i.e. "gratis".

However, in general the two concepts are not necessarily connected - one may receive gratis something which is not libre and vice versa.

The community sometimes uses the phrases "Free as in Beer" and "Free as in Speech" in place of "gratis" and "libre" - and sometimes extends this to include "Free as in Puppy", to indicate that one is accepting an obligation and ongoing costs along with the lovely pup.

Related to this is the issue of fixed costs - if someone gives me a vat of free wine, then in order for it to be useful some outlay is still required. This is essentially the question of "who pays for the bottling and labeling costs?"

However, while this is indeed an issue for physical goods, for information - where the cost of producing copies of an existing work is zero - then the equation changes considerably.

Project Gutenberg provides the scanned text of out-of-copyright works.

The role of the Standard EBooks project is to take already-sourced text and produce high quality digital editions that are both gratis and libre and which are created with fully open tools.
